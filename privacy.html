Attendance Sheet — Privacy Policy

Last updated: 3 September 2026

What this page covers

This is the digital attendance register you sign in to at an event, replacing a paper sign-in sheet. This policy explains exactly what it collects, why, who can see it, and how long it's kept — in plain language, not boilerplate. If anything here doesn't match what the system actually does, that's a bug in this policy, not a license to assume otherwise — tell the event organizer.

What is collected when you sign in

  • Your name and, optionally, your organization.
  • Your email address and/or phone number — at least one is required, both are accepted, so you can be identified as the same attendee if you ever need to correct your entry.
  • A signature you draw on-screen with your finger, stylus, or mouse.
  • An explicit yes/no answer on whether you consent to being photographed or filmed at the event. This is never assumed or defaulted — the sign-in form will not accept your entry until you've actively chosen one.

Nothing else is collected from you directly. The page does not use tracking cookies, does not run any third-party analytics or advertising script, and does not ask for your location.

Who can see your entry

This system is built around one rule: you can only ever see your own row. An ordinary attendee's device never receives anyone else's name, contact details, signature, or even the total number of people who have signed in — that count is visible only to the event's own organizer. The only people who can see the full attendee list for an event are:

  • The specific county admin account that created that event, and
  • A super admin account, for platform administration.

Every time an admin exports the list, prints it, or corrects an entry, that action is written to a tamper-evident audit log — a record of who accessed or changed what, and when, that cannot be silently edited after the fact. This exists so "who looked at this" is never just a promise.

Why this data is collected

Solely to create an official attendance record for the specific event you signed in to — the same purpose a paper sign-in sheet at the door would serve. Your email, if provided, is also used to send you a one-time confirmation of your entry and, if the organizer needs it, a secure link to add or redo your signature. It is not used for marketing, and is not shared with any third party for their own purposes.

How long your entry is kept, and correcting mistakes

Because this is meant to stand in for an official paper record, entries are not silently deleted — the same way you can't tear a page out of a physical attendance register on request. If your entry has a mistake, you (from the same device you signed in on) or the event organizer can correct it, and every correction is logged in the audit trail. An organizer can also flag or retire an entry (for example, a duplicate or a fraudulent signature) — this keeps the record honest rather than erasing it outright. If you have a concern about your own entry that you can't resolve this way, contact the event organizer using the details on the sign-in page, or the contact details below.

Where this data lives

Event and attendance data is stored in a managed Postgres database with encrypted connections. Transactional email (account activation, password resets, and your own sign-in confirmation) is sent through a third-party email delivery provider (Brevo) solely to deliver that specific message to the address you provided — it is not given access to the attendance list itself.

Kenya's Data Protection Act, 2019

This system's design choices — collecting only what's needed for attendance, requiring an explicit answer on photo/video consent rather than assuming one, restricting the attendee list to the event's own organizer, and logging who accesses or changes a record — follow the core principles of Kenya's Data Protection Act, 2019 (lawfulness, purpose limitation, data minimization, and integrity of processing). This statement describes how the system is built, not a certified legal compliance determination.
